==== *Narrative*

[.changed]#The scheduling lifecycle starts when a patient (or caregiver acting for the patient) identifies a consultation need and searches for a suitable provider. This discovery step includes comparing specialty, location, and clinic constraints, and may involve direct staff assistance when the patient cannot complete the process independently.#

[.changed]#Once a provider is selected, the patient and clinic negotiate a feasible appointment option from the provider's published `TimeSlot` availability. Each slot has a visible state (`Available`, `Held`, `Reserved`, or `Expired`), so both parties can reason about whether the slot can still be requested. This is where practical friction appears: a slot can look open, but another request may hold it before confirmation is complete.#

[.changed]#When the patient requests a slot, the system creates an `Appointment` request record instead of assuming immediate finalization. The patient may need to supply additional information at this point, such as referral evidence, insurance authorization details, or visit-type context. If prerequisites are missing, the request remains in `Requested` or `PendingApproval` until the missing condition is satisfied.#

[.changed]#This separation between request and confirmation is central to the domain model: requesting a slot is an action; confirming an appointment is a later action that may or may not succeed. A slot can be temporarily held while validation runs, then either transitions to `Reserved` (success) or returns to `Available` (rejection, expiration, or cancellation).#

[.changed]#If no acceptable slot exists, the patient may join a `Waitlist`. Later, when cancellation or newly published availability occurs, reassignment behavior evaluates eligibility and ordering rules to decide whether a waitlisted patient should be offered the opening. This avoids purely informal callback practices and makes reassignment explicit.#

[.changed]#After confirmation, the lifecycle continues operationally: reminders are issued, attendance is observed, and the appointment eventually becomes `Completed`, `Cancelled`, or `NoShow`. A cancellation releases the slot for reuse. A rescheduling action releases one slot and associates the same appointment with another slot in a single coherent transition.#

[.changed]#In short, the domain is not only about placing a patient in a time block. It is a coordinated flow that connects discovery, contact, booking, prerequisite handling, reminders, attendance outcomes, and rescheduling under real uncertainty about consult duration, no-shows, and external approvals.#
