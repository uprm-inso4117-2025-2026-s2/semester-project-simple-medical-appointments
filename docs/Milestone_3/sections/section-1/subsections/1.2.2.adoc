==== *Needs*

===== Patients

Patients are currently able to contact clinics individually, typically by phone, in order to request appointments or inquire about availability. However, this process requires repeated attempts across multiple providers, which is commonly reported in healthcare scheduling environments where appointment availability is not centralized and must be confirmed manually through staff interaction. This limits visibility into available options and depends heavily on timing and response availability from clinic staff.

This limitation is inferred from the structure of decentralized phone-based scheduling systems, where patients must sequentially contact providers rather than accessing real-time aggregated availability.

[.changed]#Patients need a reliable way to discover bookable appointments without repeated trial-and-error calls or clinic visits. For this project, this need is defined as visibility into provider, specialty, location, and slot availability, together with clear feedback when a request is pending, confirmed, canceled, or waitlisted.#

Patients therefore need a more reliable way to identify and compare viable appointment options based on factors such as specialty, location, and availability without relying on repeated manual inquiries.

===== Caregivers

[.added]#Caregivers need a way to coordinate dependent appointments for another person without losing track of linked visits, reminders, or rescheduling cascades.#

===== Clinic Administrative Staff

Clinic administrative staff are currently responsible for managing appointment scheduling through manual coordination methods, including handling phone calls, maintaining calendars, and processing changes throughout the day.

The increased administrative burden is derived from the operational characteristics of manual scheduling systems, where each appointment request, modification, or cancellation must be individually processed by staff rather than being automated or system-synchronized. While these methods allow schedules to be maintained, they require continuous attention and are prone to interruptions, errors, and inconsistencies when demand is high.

[.changed]#Clinic staff need scheduling support that reduces manual cross-checking between calls, notes, and local calendars. The need is not "automation for its own sake," but better control over slot publication, cancellation handling, waitlist follow-up, and status visibility while demand is high.#

This makes it difficult to maintain accurate schedules while simultaneously responding to patient requests. Administrative staff therefore need a way to manage scheduling activities that reduces dependence on constant manual intervention and minimizes the likelihood of coordination errors.

===== Providers

Providers are currently able to define their availability and conduct consultations according to clinic schedules. However, variability in appointment durations, patient attendance, and administrative coordination introduces uncertainty into daily operations and limits the effective use of available time. Additionally, providers often have limited visibility into how their availability is allocated or adjusted throughout the scheduling process.

[.changed]#Providers need a scheduling representation that respects operational constraints while acknowledging that consultation duration is usually estimated rather than guaranteed in advance. They also need clear visibility into how their available time has been allocated, held, confirmed, canceled, or left unused.#

Providers therefore need a way to ensure that their available time is utilized in a predictable and controlled manner while maintaining flexibility to adapt to changing conditions.

===== Clinics

[.added]#Clinics need a coherent way to define which kinds of health professionals and consultation types are bookable through the system, including any prerequisites such as referral or insurance authorization.#
