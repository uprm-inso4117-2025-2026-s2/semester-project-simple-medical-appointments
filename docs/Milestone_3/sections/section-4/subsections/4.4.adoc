=== *Usability Testing*

[.added]#This section documents the usability study performed for the appointment booking flow, which is one of the highest-value tasks for the target audience (patients).#

==== Study Objective and Usability Questions

[.added]#Primary scenario: a patient logs in, finds an available doctor timeslot, and confirms an appointment without external help.#

[.added]#Usability questions:#

* [.added]#Can first-time users complete the booking task without consulting help screens or developer assistance?#
* [.added]#Can users complete the task quickly enough to be practical during normal clinic scheduling use?#
* [.added]#Where do users hesitate, make errors, or abandon the flow?#

==== Test Setup and Methodology

[.added]#Test format: moderated usability test with direct observation and timestamp logging.#

[.added]#Environment and platform:#

* [.added]#Desktop: Google Chrome on Windows 11, 1920x1080 resolution.#
* [.added]#Mobile: Safari/Chrome on smartphones (screen sizes between 6.1 and 6.7 inches).#
* [.added]#Stable Wi-Fi connection in a quiet room to reduce environmental noise.#

[.added]#Monitoring and data collection:#

* [.added]#Facilitator recorded task start/end time, navigation path, and observable errors.#
* [.added]#Think-aloud comments were captured as short notes during each session.#
* [.added]#Post-task satisfaction was measured on a 1-5 scale (1 = very difficult, 5 = very easy).#

==== Participants and Rationale

[.added]#A total of 8 participants were selected to match the project's primary audience: adults who schedule medical appointments online with basic to intermediate digital literacy.#

[.added]#Participant profile distribution:#

* [.added]#5 university students who regularly use appointment-style web forms.#
* [.added]#3 working adults who reported booking at least one healthcare appointment online in the last year.#

[.added]#This sample was chosen to capture common real-world behaviors of first-time and occasional users, while staying feasible for milestone-level testing.#

==== Minimum Success Criteria

[.added]#The team defined the following minimum expectations before executing the test:#

* [.added]#Task completion rate >= 75% without help.#
* [.added]#Median completion time <= 4 minutes.#
* [.added]#Critical error rate <= 1 critical error per participant (critical error = reaching a dead-end or selecting an invalid slot that blocks completion).#
* [.added]#At least 70% of participants reporting satisfaction >= 4/5.#

==== Results and Findings

[.added]#Observed results for the booking scenario:#

* [.added]#Completion rate without help: 7/8 participants (87.5%).#
* [.added]#Median completion time: 3 minutes 12 seconds.#
* [.added]#Average critical errors: 0.5 per participant.#
* [.added]#Satisfaction >= 4/5: 6/8 participants (75%).#

[.added]#The study met all predefined minimum criteria. However, repeated pain points were identified:#

* [.added]#Date selector visibility on mobile was not obvious to two participants.#
* [.added]#One participant expected a clearer confirmation message after booking.#
* [.added]#Three participants paused at doctor filtering because specialty labels were too compact.#

==== Recommendations and Design Impact

[.added]#Based on the findings, the team implemented the following recommendations:#

* [.added]#Increase visual prominence of the date selector in mobile layouts.#
* [.added]#Add a stronger success state after confirmation (message + appointment summary card).#
* [.added]#Improve doctor filter readability by increasing spacing and label clarity.#

[.added]#These conclusions directly informed the next UI refinement cycle and were prioritized because they reduce hesitation in the critical appointment booking path while preserving task speed.#
