== *Descriptive Part*
=== *Domain Description*

==== *Domain Rough Sketches*

===== Raw Notes from Problem Context

* Demand for medical appointments exceeds supply in multiple regions.
* Physician migration has reduced appointment availability and increased clinic workload.
* Patients frequently wait weeks or months for non-urgent consultations.
* Phone-based scheduling remains the dominant method in many clinics.
* Appointment cancellations are often redistributed informally rather than systematically.

===== Stakeholder Fragments

====== Patients

* “You call and nobody answers.”
* “Sometimes they give you an appointment months away.”
* “I take whatever appointment they give me even if transportation is difficult.”
* Elderly patients often depend on relatives to manage appointments.
* Some patients maintain lists of clinics to call repeatedly.
* Transportation limitations influence which appointments patients accept.

====== Clinic Administrative Staff

* Phones ring continuously during working hours.
* Appointment notebooks, spreadsheets, or whiteboards are still used.
* Cancelations are communicated verbally and may not reach other patients.
* Walk-ins must be balanced against scheduled appointments.
* Reminder messages are sometimes sent manually from staff phones.

====== Doctors / Providers

* Providers report overloaded daily schedules.
* Appointments frequently run longer than planned.
* Administrative coordination reduces time available for care.
* Specialists receive referrals without visibility into real waiting times.
* Providers may prioritize returning patients over new patients.
* Some physicians split time across multiple clinics.

===== Observations About Clinic Operations

* Hybrid scheduling practices observed:
** phone calls
** paper calendars
** spreadsheets
** individual reminder messages
* Appointment duration varies widely depending on consultation type.
* Walk-in visits remain common, especially outside metropolitan areas.
* No-show rates fluctuate and are difficult for clinics to predict.

===== Environmental and Systemic Factors

* Healthcare access varies by municipality and transportation availability.
* Insurance authorization processes can delay appointment confirmation.
* Referral requirements introduce additional coordination steps.
* Scheduling is frequently restricted to office hours.
* Digital literacy differs across patient age groups.
* Aging population contributes to recurring appointment demand.

===== Anecdotal Patterns Observed

* Patients contact multiple clinics simultaneously seeking earlier slots.
* Staff sometimes maintain informal priority lists.
* Families coordinate multiple appointments for relatives.
* Some clinics stop scheduling once daily slots fill.
* Cancelations are occasionally reassigned through personal contacts.

===== Domain Tensions (Unprocessed Notes)

* Limited provider availability vs. high patient demand
* Manual coordination vs. unpredictable schedules
* Need for flexibility vs. administrative overload
* Regional inequality in service access
* Dependence on informal communication channels
