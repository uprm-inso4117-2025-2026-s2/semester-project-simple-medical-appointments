=== [.added]#*Test Scenario Design*#

[.added]#This section documents the structured approach used to define test scenarios that capture expected system behavior. Scenarios are grounded in functional requirements and domain concepts identified in Sections 3.1 and 3.2.#

==== [.added]#*Scenario Coverage Overview*#

[.added]#Test scenarios were derived from the core domain events identified in Section 3.1: Appointment Request, Confirmation, Cancellation, Rescheduling, No-Show, and Walk-In Arrival. Coverage was organized to ensure each functional requirement has at least one corresponding behavioral scenario.#

[.added]#Scenarios address both standard flows (happy paths) and exception flows (edge cases such as referral expiration, insurance denial, and double-booking conflicts).#

==== [.added]#*Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) Scenario Design*#

[.added]#BDD scenarios were written using the Given–When–Then structure to clearly define preconditions, triggering actions, and verifiable outcomes. This format ensures that scenarios remain readable to both technical and non-technical stakeholders while providing precise behavioral specifications.#

[.added]#*Given–When–Then Structure*#

[.added]#Each scenario follows this structure:#

* [.added]#*Given* – the initial system state or preconditions (e.g., a patient is logged in, a time slot is available).#
* [.added]#*When* – the action or event that triggers the behavior (e.g., the patient submits a booking request).#
* [.added]#*Then* – the expected observable outcome (e.g., the slot is marked reserved and a confirmation is issued).#

[.added]#*Example Scenarios*#

[.added]#The following scenarios illustrate how BDD was applied to core system behaviors:#

[.added]#*Scenario 1 – Successful Appointment Booking*#

----
Given a registered patient is authenticated
  And at least one time slot is available for the selected provider
When the patient submits an appointment booking request
Then the selected time slot is marked as reserved
  And a confirmation notification is sent to the patient
  And the appointment appears in the provider's schedule
----

[.added]#*Scenario 2 – Appointment Rescheduling*#

----
Given a patient has a confirmed appointment
  And an alternative available time slot exists
When the patient requests to reschedule the appointment
Then the original time slot is released
  And the new time slot is reserved
  And an updated confirmation is sent to the patient
----

[.added]#*Scenario 3 – Appointment Cancellation*#

----
Given a patient has a confirmed appointment
When the patient cancels the appointment
Then the appointment is marked as cancelled
  And the time slot becomes available for other patients
  And a cancellation notification is sent to both patient and provider
----

[.added]#*Scenario 4 – Booking Rejected Due to Unavailability*#

----
Given a patient is authenticated
  And no time slots are available for the selected provider on the requested date
When the patient attempts to book an appointment
Then the system rejects the booking request
  And an informational message is displayed indicating no availability
----

[.added]#*Scenario 5 – No-Show Registration*#

----
Given a confirmed appointment exists
  And the appointment time has passed
  And the patient did not attend
When the provider or system marks the appointment as a no-show
Then the appointment status is updated to No-Show
  And the time slot record is preserved for administrative reporting
----
